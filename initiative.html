Future direction · not part of the first pilot

Six in ten referred women never reach colposcopy. The screening already found them.

Bangladesh’s national programme screened 1.56 million women in five years and recorded every positive result. Then it loses most of them at the next step. The Prevention Adoption Initiative is a weekly routine for that step: find who is overdue, rank by urgency, act with a call and a booking and the fare, close when it is done. This page shows how it could run here, first on cancer screening and then on the chronic conditions a corporate check-up finds, who would do what, and who would pay. The design is published; the first pilot is in Michigan; this is the Bangladesh adaptation, ready for a first conversation.

How it moves · every stage ends with its own number
  1. Michigan, one clinicThree months of feasibility, then the clinic decides whether to go to month twelve. An independent evaluator, once one is confirmed, judges against the clinic’s own trend; without one, the result is the clinic’s own before-and-after.
  2. Bangladesh, one providerModel A: one provider, one factory client, three months, then the same decision Michigan makes: stop, or continue to twelve months. The evaluator here is a university or research institute, named before the start (see 06).
  3. Then the programmeModel C goes to the national programme on model A’s number, not before. Each stage is judged by the number it set out to move.

Nessa et al., BMC Global and Public Health 2025;3:34, from the national DHIS2 case-based registry, which already sends each positive woman an SMS. The same shape appears outside cancer: 32% of new NCD-corner patients lost within three months, mostly to medicine stock-outs, transport cost and queues (BMC Health Services Research, Jan 2026); BRAC’s NCD360 could track 22% of the patients it referred into government facilities (Daily Waadaa, Sep 2026); factory VIA clinics treat about half of abnormals on site and refer the rest, with nothing published on the referred half (Jhpiego, Jan 2026).

Nobody publishes a completion rate for factory or check-up referrals.The national programme reports one five-year figure, 40.4% to colposcopy. For women referred from a factory camp or a corporate check-up, how many reached the next step is not published. That is the number this routine produces.
02 · The routine

Two hours a week, one short list, four steps.

The provider’s own nurse runs it with a worklist. HSREP keeps the list and the rules. An independent evaluator judges the number. No software to buy, no new staff.

  1. 01 · Find

    Everyone who was told to get a test and has no result recorded.

  2. 02 · Rank

    Days overdue, days since a positive result, prior no-shows. Three rules, no model.

  3. 03 · Act

    A call in the patient’s language, a kit, a booking, help with transport.

  4. 04 · Close

    The test is done, and the days from referral to result are counted.

Invented patient · what one row looks likeRina B. · VIA-positive at a factory screening camp · 70 days, no colposcopy → called in Bangla, booked at the medical college colposcopy clinic, fare sent by bKash, reminder the day before, a paid morning off agreed with the factory → done on day 76.

Paid per completion, never per referral.Doctors paid a commission for each test have no reason to chase a patient who already paid, and patients who suspect padded tests have a reason not to come back. In this routine the outreach nurse is paid for the list, and the provider is paid when the woman finishes.
03 · Three ways it could run

Same routine, three places to put it.

In order. The first is small enough to start with one provider and one factory client, and it is where the money for screening is already being spent.

A · Start here

The referral tail of factory screening

A private provider already screens garment workers in the factory and refers the women it cannot treat on site. The routine follows those women to the public colposcopy clinic.

The list
The provider’s own register of referred women. The factory never sees a result.
Who acts
The provider’s outreach nurse, two hours a week. The factory doctor or welfare officer releases the right women on the right days.
The test
Free at the public colposcopy clinic. Private only where the public clinic is far or slow, at a fixed price.
Who pays
The sponsor that already pays for the screening, for example a brand programme, UNFPA, or the factory as a corporate client. A small fee per completed follow-up, plus the fare.
MeasureShare of referred women reaching colposcopy within 90 days, against the national 40.4%.
B · Alongside A

A completion contract on corporate check-ups

A network already sells annual check-ups to employers. Every round produces high blood pressure readings, HbA1c over the line, abnormal creatinine. Then nothing happens. The routine follows each abnormal result to its next step inside the network.

The list
Abnormal results from the network’s own corporate check-ups with no next step recorded. See the finish lines below.
Who acts
Its outreach staff, paid for the list. No doctor is paid for the referral. One transparent price for the next step.
The test
In-house: a repeat reading, a confirmatory test, a doctor visit, the yearly eye and kidney tests.
Who pays
The network, and the employer through the check-up contract. It is buying completion from people it already tested, and a product every employer can buy: completion, not a check-up.
MeasureShare of abnormal results that reached their next step within the window, per employer.
C · After A

A follow-up cell inside the national programme

Two or three upazilas use the registry’s existing list of VIA-positive women and add what the SMS cannot do.

The list
Already in the DHIS2 tracker, with a national ID.
Who acts
The community clinic’s own provider: a phone call, a booked slot, the fare; colposcopy and histopathology written back.
The test
Public, with contracted private capacity at a fixed price where the public clinic is overloaded.
Who pays
It could come from programme money already committed for 2027–2031, the EIB Women’s Health Initiative with UNFPA technical assistance. None of it is agreed for this.
MeasureThe national 40.4% in those upazilas, a year later, and the share of treatments with histopathology on record.

Each stage ends with its own number, judged by an independent evaluator named before the start, and the next stage begins on that number.

03b · Beyond cancer screening

The same routine on a corporate check-up: three conditions, six finish lines.

Chronic disease is where the corporate check-up finds the most abnormal results and does the least about them. Each line below is a recommended step that people start and do not finish, with the size of the condition in Bangladesh and the finish line the pilot would count. Stress and lifestyle are real causes and bad finish lines: they belong in the Act step, never in the measure.

  1. 01 · Hypertension · first

    21% of adults have raised blood pressure; nationally about 14% of those with it are controlled, and the public programme that tracks every patient reaches 58%.

    Finish lines: a repeat reading on another day and a doctor visit, within the month; a repeat reading at 90 days after treatment starts.

  2. 02 · Diabetes · second

    13% of adults, about 13.9 million people, and 39% of them do not know. HbA1c is already in the check-up.

    Finish lines: a second test to confirm, with a doctor visit, within the month; HbA1c at 90 days after treatment starts; for everyone already diagnosed, the eye exam and the urine-albumin and eGFR check, at least yearly.

  3. 03 · Kidney · rides on the panel

    About 22% of adults in pooled studies, almost always found late. Creatinine is already in the panel, so an abnormal result costs nothing extra to find.

    Finish line: a repeat creatinine and urine-albumin test to confirm; chronic means abnormal for three months or more.

  4. For factories · anaemia

    About eight in ten female garment workers are anaemic in published samples. Haemoglobin is cheap and pairs with the VIA camp.

    Finish line: a repeat haemoglobin after three months of iron.

Raised blood pressure 21.0% and raised blood glucose 8.3%, adults 18–69: WHO STEPS Bangladesh 2018. Control about 14% nationally and 58% in the Bangladesh Hypertension Control Initiative, 328 facilities, March 2026: National Heart Foundation. Diabetes 13.2% of adults 20–79, 13.9 million, 39.1% undiagnosed, 2024: IDF Diabetes Atlas. CKD 22.48% pooled: Banik et al., Int Urol Nephrol 2021. Anaemia in female garment workers: Hossain et al., Nutrients 2019. Finish lines follow the Bangladesh National Hypertension Guideline, the ADA Standards of Care and KDIGO 2024; the windows (the month, 90 days) are the pilot’s own and would be set with the clinical lead.

Declined for now: lipids on their own (the next step is a risk decision, not a test), stress and mental health (no finish line a nurse can close), fatty liver, tobacco.

05 · In plain words

What I would say in the room.

If you run a private networkYou already screen factory workers. Around six in a hundred have an abnormal result; some you treat that day, the rest you refer, and then nobody knows. Your nurse keeps the list of referred women and works it two hours a week: call, book, send the fare, remind, write down when it is done. The follow-up cases come to you by contract, and you get a number no one else here has. One network, three months, one factory client, then a decision: stop, or continue to twelve months.
If you fund factory screeningYour screening finds the women. The programme loses them at the next step: nationally, four in ten reach colposcopy. Add a small fee per completed follow-up and the fare. The test itself is free at the public clinic. You get, for the first time, a completion rate per factory instead of a screening count.
If you run the national programmeYour registry already lists every VIA-positive woman and sends her an SMS. In two or three upazilas, add a phone call from the community clinic, a booked slot, the fare, and the result written back, including histopathology. The measure is your own 40.4%, in those upazilas, a year later.

The one-page brief (PDF)

Experience in one setting does not show that the routine will work in another. Bangladesh is where I already have standing and screening-service experience; that is a reason to start here one day, not evidence that it would work here. The first pilot in Michigan, its evaluation and its published toolkit come first. Figures above are from the sources named; none is HSREP’s own data.

All of this depends on the first pilot.
If you run a provider, fund factory screening, or sit inside the programme, and you want to talk about the step after the referral, that is the conversation to have.
